The Fun Maths Corner

Maths isn't just about exams — it's full of brilliant puzzles, games and tricks. Here are a few favourites I love sharing with students. Have a go below!

The 18 Lines Game

Start with 18 lines. Taking turns, each player crosses off 1, 2 or 3 lines. Whoever is forced to cross off the very last line loses.

It looks like luck, but it's not — it's all about staying in control of the remainder after dividing by 4. Play a few rounds and see if you can work out the rule before the computer beats you to it!

You go first. Choose how many lines to cross off (1, 2 or 3).

Your turn — cross off 1, 2 or 3 lines.

Unbeatable Noughts & Crosses

With perfect play, noughts and crosses (tic-tac-toe) always ends in a draw — nobody can force a win. The computer below plays perfectly using an algorithm called minimax, which looks ahead through every possible sequence of moves to pick the best one.

The Towers of Hanoi

Move the whole stack of discs from the left peg to the right peg, one disc at a time, never placing a larger disc on top of a smaller one.

The minimum number of moves for n discs is 2n − 1. Legend says monks are solving a 64-disc version of this puzzle — at one move per second, that would take over 580 billion years!
